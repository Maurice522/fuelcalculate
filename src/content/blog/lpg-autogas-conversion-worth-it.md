LPG, often marketed as Autogas, tends to get less attention than CNG or EV in fuel-switching conversations, but it follows a similar logic: usually cheap to run, with an upfront and practical cost that determines whether that saving is actually worth capturing.

## The running-cost case

LPG is typically priced well below petrol, and converted vehicles generally retain a reasonable share of their petrol-equivalent efficiency — the mileage loss from running on LPG is usually more modest than what you'd see with some other alternative fuels. For anyone doing meaningful distance, the combination of a lower price and a manageable efficiency trade-off usually adds up to a real saving per kilometre.

## What determines whether it pays off

Three factors decide whether an LPG conversion is worth it for your situation:

- **Conversion cost.** LPG kits and fitting have a real upfront cost, which needs to be earned back through fuel savings before it's a net benefit — the more you drive, the faster that happens.
- **Tank space.** Like CNG, the LPG tank typically takes up boot space. Whether that's a meaningful trade-off depends on how you use the vehicle.
- **Station availability.** LPG/Autogas stations are less common than petrol pumps, though generally more widespread than CNG in areas where it's established. Check realistically whether your normal routes pass near one — a saving that requires regular detours to capture is a smaller saving than it looks on paper.

## Who it tends to make sense for

LPG conversion tends to be worth it most clearly for higher-mileage drivers in areas with decent Autogas availability, and for owners planning to keep the vehicle long enough for the conversion cost to be fully offset by running-cost savings. It's a weaker case for low-mileage use, where the payback period can stretch out longer than it's practical to plan around.
