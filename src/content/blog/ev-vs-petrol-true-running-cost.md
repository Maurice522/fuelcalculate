The headline claim about EVs — that they're cheaper to run than petrol cars — is generally true, and by a wide margin in most cases. But "generally true" isn't the same as "true for your specific numbers," and the gap between the two is worth understanding before you treat it as settled.

## Why the running-cost gap is usually large

Electricity is typically far cheaper per kilometre than petrol, for a straightforward reason: electric motors convert energy to motion far more efficiently than internal combustion engines, which lose a large share of fuel energy as heat. Combined with electricity generally costing less per unit of energy than petrol, the result is a running-cost gap that tends to favour EVs clearly, not marginally.

## What actually determines the size of the gap

Two numbers do all the work here, and both vary far more than fuel prices do:

- **Your electricity tariff.** Home charging rates, time-of-use tariffs, and public charging prices can differ substantially — a home off-peak rate and a public fast-charger rate aren't the same calculation at all.
- **Your EV's efficiency.** Measured as energy used per 100 km, this varies by vehicle, driving style, and conditions (cold weather in particular increases energy use). A generic "EVs are cheap to run" claim glosses over the fact that two different EVs can have meaningfully different running costs.

## What this comparison doesn't capture

Running cost per kilometre is only one part of the ownership decision. It doesn't include:

- The typically higher upfront price of an EV compared to an equivalent petrol vehicle.
- Home-charging setup cost, if you don't already have one.
- Charging time and convenience versus a petrol fill-up, which matters more for some driving patterns than others.

None of these make the running-cost advantage less real — they're just separate questions that a per-km comparison doesn't answer on its own. The honest way to use a running-cost number is as one input into a bigger decision, including how many years of driving it takes for fuel savings to offset any price premium.
