Anyone who has driven between Indian states and filled up along the way has probably noticed the pump price change — sometimes by a small amount, sometimes by more than feels like it should for the same fuel. This isn't inconsistent pricing; it's a predictable result of how fuel is taxed and priced in India.

## VAT is set state by state

The largest driver of city-to-city price differences is that VAT (value-added tax) on fuel is set by individual state governments, not nationally. Two cities in different states can have a genuinely different tax rate applied to the same base fuel price, and that difference shows up directly at the pump. This is also why prices don't move uniformly across the country when the base price changes — the state tax component means the actual pump-price shift can differ from state to state even for the same underlying change.

## Local factors add smaller differences

Beyond state tax, smaller factors contribute too — transportation distance from refineries or depots, local dealer commissions, and daily price-revision timing can all cause minor variation even between cities in the same state. These effects are generally smaller than the VAT difference but still real.

## Why a national average is the wrong number for your budget

If you're calculating your own fuel costs using a national average price rather than your actual city's price, you're working with a number that may not describe your reality at all — the gap between the cheapest and most expensive major cities can be substantial. For budgeting purposes, this matters more than it might seem: a price difference of even a few percent, applied across a full month of driving, is a real gap in your actual spend versus your estimate.

## It also matters for fuel-switching decisions

If you're weighing a switch to CNG, LPG, or an EV based on the cost comparison, the size of the saving depends partly on your local petrol or diesel price — the same percentage saving looks different in absolute terms depending on your city's baseline price. Using your own city's number, not a generic figure, gives you an honest picture of whether a switch is actually worth it for you specifically.
