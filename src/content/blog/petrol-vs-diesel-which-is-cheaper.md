"Diesel is cheaper" and "petrol is cheaper" are both true statements, depending on which part of the equation you're looking at. The pump price is only half the picture — mileage is the other half, and it's where most petrol-vs-diesel comparisons go wrong.

## Why pump price alone is misleading

Diesel is often priced below petrol per litre, which is where the "diesel is cheaper" assumption comes from. But diesel and petrol engines don't return the same mileage even in the same vehicle class — diesel engines are generally more fuel-efficient, especially at highway speeds. So the real comparison isn't "which fuel costs less per litre," it's "which fuel costs less per kilometre for this specific vehicle," and those can point in different directions.

## Where each one tends to win

Broadly speaking:

- **Diesel tends to pull ahead** on longer, steadier drives — highway commutes, frequent long-distance trips — where its efficiency advantage has room to show up, and where the pump-price gap adds a second advantage on top of it.
- **Petrol tends to hold up better** for short, stop-start city driving, where diesel's efficiency edge shrinks and the vehicle's higher upfront cost (diesel vehicles are usually pricier to buy and service) has less running-cost advantage to offset it over a realistic ownership period.

Neither of these is a rule — it depends entirely on your specific vehicle's mileage figures on each fuel, and your actual driving mix.

## The maintenance side people forget

Running cost isn't only fuel. Diesel vehicles typically cost more to service, and diesel particulate filters and injection systems can be pricier to repair than their petrol equivalents. If you're comparing two different vehicles (not the same model in both fuel variants), factor this in separately — a diesel that wins narrowly on fuel cost can lose overall once maintenance is included, particularly for low-mileage drivers who never get enough distance to earn back the fuel savings.

## How to actually settle it for your situation

The only way to get a real answer is to plug in your own numbers rather than rely on a rule of thumb:

1. Get your vehicle's mileage on each fuel — either from your own logged experience or a reliable per-model source.
2. Enter your actual local fuel prices for each — not a national average, since the petrol-diesel price gap varies by location.
3. Use your real typical distance, not a round number.
