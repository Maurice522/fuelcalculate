For a small business running even a handful of vehicles — delivery vans, service vehicles, a small taxi fleet — fuel is usually one of the larger controllable operating costs. "Controllable" is the key word: unlike rent or wages, fuel cost responds directly to route planning, vehicle choice, and driving behaviour, which makes tracking it properly worth the effort.

## Why per-vehicle tracking beats a fleet-wide average

A common shortcut is estimating fuel cost as one average figure across the whole fleet. This hides real differences: an older vehicle with worse mileage, a driver with a more fuel-heavy driving style, or a route that includes more idle time will all cost more per kilometre than the fleet average suggests — and averaging them together means the underperforming vehicle or route never gets flagged as a problem worth fixing. Tracking cost per kilometre per vehicle, not just total fuel spend, is what actually surfaces where the money is going.

## Fuel type mix matters more at fleet scale

A cost-per-km difference that looks small for one vehicle becomes significant multiplied across a fleet and a full year of operation. If some vehicles could realistically run on CNG, LPG, or electric instead of petrol or diesel, the potential saving is worth calculating properly rather than dismissing based on a general impression — at fleet scale, even a modest per-km saving adds up to a real number.

## Route and idle time are part of the real cost

For delivery or service fleets, idle time (waiting at a stop, in traffic) burns fuel without covering distance, the same issue that affects individual delivery drivers but multiplied across every vehicle in the fleet. Route planning that reduces unnecessary idling or backtracking is a direct, controllable lever on fuel cost — more so than most other cost-saving measures available to a small operation.

## A practical tracking approach

Rather than an annual guess, calculate real mileage per vehicle periodically (fuel used divided by distance covered, from odometer or trip logs) and treat any vehicle whose real mileage is notably worse than the others as a flag worth investigating — it might need maintenance, or the assigned route might genuinely be less efficient.
