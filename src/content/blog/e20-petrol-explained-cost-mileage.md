If you've filled up at an Indian petrol pump recently, there's a good chance what went into your tank was E20, not pure petrol — the 20% ethanol blend has become the default at most stations. Most drivers have noticed the name without knowing exactly what it changes.

## What E20 actually is

E20 is petrol blended with 20% ethanol. It's part of a national push toward higher ethanol blending, and unlike some fuel changes, this one didn't require you to opt in — it's simply what "petrol" now means at most pumps. The practical questions that matter to a driver are: is my car compatible, does it cost more or less, and does it affect my mileage.

## Compatibility first

Most petrol cars manufactured in India since 2023 are E20-compatible by default, and many older BS-VI petrol vehicles can run on it too. If you're unsure, check your owner's manual or the fuel-filler cap for an E20-compatible label. If your vehicle isn't explicitly rated for E20, it's worth checking with the manufacturer before assuming it's fine — ethanol blends above E10 can affect fuel lines and seals in some older engines over time.

## The mileage trade-off

Ethanol has a lower calorific value than petrol, which means E20 typically returns somewhat lower mileage than pure petrol — commonly cited estimates put the drop around 6%, though the real number varies by engine and driving style. This isn't a flaw specific to your car; it's a property of the fuel itself.

## Does it actually cost you more?

This is the part that surprises people: not necessarily. E20 is usually priced a little below pure petrol, which can offset some or most of the mileage drop. Whether you come out ahead, behind, or roughly even depends on the exact price gap in your area and your vehicle's specific efficiency loss — there's no single answer that applies to every car.
