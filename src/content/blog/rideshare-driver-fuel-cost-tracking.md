For a casual driver, fuel cost is a household budget line. For a rideshare or cab driver, it's a direct cost against your actual earnings — every kilometre driven without a fare, every bit of idling in traffic waiting for a pickup, comes straight out of your margin. That makes an accurate number far more important than a rough estimate.

## Why "average" numbers don't work for this

Most fuel cost guidance assumes a fairly predictable commute: same route, similar distance, most days. Rideshare driving is the opposite — variable routes, unpredictable idle time between fares, and a mix of short city hops and occasional longer trips. A single "average cost per km" figure calculated once tends to drift out of date fast, because your actual driving pattern that week might look nothing like the week before.

## The idle-time problem

Time spent waiting for a fare with the engine running (traffic, AC, or just circling for a pickup) burns fuel without covering any distance that shows up in a per-km calculation. This is easy to underestimate because it doesn't feel like "driving" the way a trip does, but it's real fuel cost that a simple distance-based estimate misses. If your idle time is significant, your real cost per completed trip is higher than a pure distance calculation suggests.

## Tracking it properly instead of guessing

The most reliable approach is to periodically calculate your real mileage directly — litres filled divided by distance covered since the last fill-up — rather than relying on a manufacturer figure or an old estimate. Doing this regularly (weekly, if your driving pattern varies a lot) catches changes in your actual efficiency, whether from traffic conditions, vehicle wear, or a shift in your driving pattern, that a one-time calculation would miss entirely.

## Comparing fuel types if you're deciding on a vehicle

If fuel is a meaningful cost against your earnings, the fuel type you're driving matters more than it would for a casual driver. CNG and EV both typically cost significantly less per kilometre than petrol, which can make a real difference to take-home earnings for someone doing high daily distance. The trade-offs — CNG's more limited station network, an EV's charging time and upfront cost — matter more too, precisely because you're relying on the vehicle for income rather than convenience.
