Most EV cost conversations focus on the running-cost saving, and it's real — but the first year of owning an EV has a different cost shape than a petrol car's, with some expenses front-loaded and others simply not present at all. Understanding that shape matters more than a single "EVs are cheaper" headline.

## The running-cost saving shows up immediately

Unlike some cost benefits that take years to materialise, the per-kilometre saving from switching to electricity is immediate — from your first charge, you're very likely paying less per kilometre than you were on petrol. How large that saving is depends on your electricity tariff and your EV's efficiency, both of which are worth checking against your actual numbers rather than assuming a generic figure.

## What's different in year one specifically

A few costs behave differently in the first year compared to ongoing ownership:

- **Home charging setup**, if needed, is typically a one-time cost concentrated at the start of ownership, not spread across the year. It's real money, but it's not a recurring expense the way fuel is.
- **No fuel-pump routine** changes daily behaviour more than budgeting — charging at home overnight means the "trip to the pump" simply stops being a thing, which is a convenience shift as much as a cost one.
- **Maintenance costs are typically lower** in the first year (no oil changes, fewer moving parts to service) but this is also the year furthest from any longer-term battery-related costs that might appear later in ownership — first-year maintenance savings shouldn't be assumed to represent the full-ownership picture.

## The upfront price gap is still the biggest variable

EVs are generally priced higher upfront than an equivalent petrol vehicle, and that gap is the main thing the running-cost saving has to work against. How many years it takes for fuel savings to close that gap depends entirely on how much you drive — a high-mileage driver reaches break-even far sooner than an occasional one, which means the "is it worth it" answer genuinely differs by driver, not just by vehicle.

## Building your own first-year comparison

Rather than relying on a general claim, work out your specific numbers: your current petrol cost per km using your real mileage and price, against an EV's cost per km using a realistic tariff and efficiency figure.
